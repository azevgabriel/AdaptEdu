# Brazilian Legislation & Accessibility Guidelines for Inclusive Education

This document establishes the centralized normative baseline and legal reference guide for AdaptEdu. It details the Brazilian constitutional and federal legislative framework governing inclusive education, outlines statutory educational entitlements for neurodivergent students and students with disabilities, and provides the architectural mapping matrix connecting legal mandates to AdaptEdu's current and roadmap platform modules.

---

## 🎯 Purpose & Normative Scope

Brazilian public school educators operate under demanding classroom conditions, frequently without dedicated pedagogical training in legal accessibility entitlements. AdaptEdu bridges this gap by translating statutory accessibility mandates into practical, low-friction digital tools and classroom workflows.

Every feature, AI pedagogical recommendation, and user experience pattern in AdaptEdu must strictly align with the legal standards detailed herein.

> [!IMPORTANT]
> **Hypothesis-Only & Non-Diagnostic Principle:**
> All pedagogical insights, screening indicators, and adaptation recommendations generated by AdaptEdu operate strictly under the non-diagnostic principle defined in [AGENTS.md](AGENTS.md). The platform **NEVER** issues clinical or medical diagnoses. Technological assistance serves to identify observational hypotheses based on educational literature and Brazilian legislation, empowering teachers to collaborate with school coordination, families, and specialized healthcare professionals.

## 📚 Legal & Regulatory Framework (Brazilian Federal Legislation)

Brazilian inclusive education is anchored by constitutional amendments, international treaties, federal laws, and regulatory decrees. Below are the key statutory pillars that govern accessibility, reasonable accommodation, and pedagogical rights in educational institutions.

### 1. Right to Accessibility Services, Permanence, and School Support Professionals

- **Core Right:** Students with disabilities and neurodivergent learners have the guaranteed legal right to an inclusive educational system at all levels, accompanied by accessibility services and resources that secure access, permanence, full participation, and learning without discrimination.
- **Individualized Support & Dedicated Support Professionals:** Schools must provide individualized and collective support measures in environments that maximize academic and social development. When verified necessary, the student is entitled to a dedicated school support professional (*profissional de apoio escolar*) within the regular classroom at no additional cost to families.
- **Statutory Citations:**
  - **Decreto Federal nº 6.949/2009:** Promulgates the *Convenção Internacional sobre os Direitos das Pessoas com Deficiência (CDPD)* and its Optional Protocol. Ratified under the procedure of Art. 5, § 3 of the Brazilian Federal Constitution, holding the legal status of a **Constitutional Amendment** (*Emenda Constitucional*).
    - *Art. 24, item 2, alínea "d":* Persons with disabilities must receive the necessary support within the general education system to facilitate their effective education.
    - *Art. 24, item 2, alínea "e":* Effective individualized support measures must be provided in environments that maximize academic and social development, consistent with the goal of full inclusion.
  - **Lei Federal nº 13.146/2015 (Lei Brasileira de Inclusão da Pessoa com Deficiência - LBI / Estatuto da Pessoa com Deficiência):**
    - *Art. 28, Inciso I:* Mandates an inclusive educational system at all levels and lifelong learning.
    - *Art. 28, Inciso II:* Mandates the creation of an inclusive pedagogical project (*projeto pedagógico*) that institutionalizes quality educational support services and resources.
    - *Art. 28, Inciso III:* Requires reasonable adaptation, accessibility, and individual support plans (*Plano de Atendimento Educacional Especializado / Plano de Desenvolvimento Individual*).
    - *Art. 28, Inciso XVII:* Guarantees the provision of a dedicated school support professional (*profissional de apoio escolar*).
  - **Lei Federal nº 12.764/2012 (Política Nacional de Proteção dos Direitos da Pessoa com Transtorno do Espectro Autista - TEA):**
    - *Art. 3º, Parágrafo Único:* In cases of proven need, students with Autism Spectrum Disorder (TEA) enrolled in regular schools are explicitly entitled to a specialized school support professional (*acompanhante especializado*).

### 2. Instruction in Libras, Braille, and Assistive Technology

- **Core Right:** Students with disabilities have the statutory right to receive instruction in Brazilian Sign Language (*Língua Brasileira de Sinais - Libras*), the Braille tactile system, and assistive technology (*tecnologia assistiva*) resources to expand their functional capacities, autonomy, communication, and full classroom participation.
- **Statutory Citations:**
  - **Lei Federal nº 13.146/2015 (LBI):**
    - *Art. 28, Inciso IV:* Mandates the early and continuous instruction of Libras, the Braille system, and the use of assistive technology resources to maximize functional potential.
    - *Art. 28, Inciso XII:* Mandates accessibility in all educational facilities, including digital resources, websites, and pedagogical materials.
    - *Art. 28, Inciso XIV:* Guarantees the adoption of individual and collective pedagogical measures and inclusive didactic materials.
  - **Lei Federal nº 9.394/1996 (Diretrizes e Bases da Educação Nacional - LDB):**
    - *Art. 60-A:* Details Specialized Educational Services (*Atendimento Educacional Especializado - AEE*), guaranteeing institutionalized support, assistive technology, and specialized pedagogical techniques across public and private systems.

---

### 3. Complex Communication Needs & Augmentative and Alternative Communication (AAC / CAA)

- **Core Right:** Students with complex communication needs (including non-speaking or minimally speaking autistic students, students with cerebral palsy, developmental delays, or expressive language challenges) are legally entitled to receive Augmentative and Alternative Communication (AAC / *Comunicação Aumentativa e Alternativa - CAA*) resources.
- **Low-Tech Priority:** The law explicitly emphasizes the provision and pedagogical integration of low-tech assistive communication tools, specifically including communication boards with pictograms (*pranchas com pictogramas*), enabling seamless interaction within Specialized Educational Services (AEE) and the regular classroom.
- **Statutory Citations:**
  - **Lei Federal nº 13.146/2015 (LBI):**
    - *Art. 28, Inciso XIX:* Explicitly establishes the duty of the educational system to ensure the provision of augmentative and alternative communication resources, specifically including communication boards with pictograms (*pranchas de comunicação com pictogramas*), ensuring non-verbal students can communicate needs, engage in curriculum tasks, and interact with peers and teachers.

---

### 4. Accessibility in Selective Processes & Vocational / Higher Education

- **Core Right:** All assessment mechanisms, selective admissions, vestibular processes, national examinations (e.g., ENEM), and vocational/technical education selection systems must provide full accessibility for candidates with disabilities and neurodivergent conditions.
- **Mandatory Accommodations:**
  - Public notice published in Libras (*edital em Libras*).
  - Accessible examination formats: screen-reader compatible digital files, large print (*formato ampliado*), super-large print, and Braille.
  - Time extension (*dilação de tempo / tempo adicional*) for completing exams.
  - Accessibility resources and human proctors: Libras translators and interpreters, transcription proctors (*ledores e transcritores*), quiet rooms, and ergonomic adjustments requested in advance.
- **Statutory Citations:**
  - **Lei Federal nº 13.146/2015 (LBI):**
    - *Art. 28, Inciso VI:* Requires adoption of accessible evaluation criteria and mechanisms in selective processes.
    - *Art. 30:* Establishes mandatory accessibility in selective processes for higher education and vocational/technological education (incisos I through VI: accessible notices, format options, time extensions, and customized assistive resources).
  - **Lei Federal nº 9.394/1996 (LDB):**
    - *Capítulo V-A:* Emphasizes continuity of inclusive education into technical, technological, and higher education.

---

### 5. Libras as First Language & Bilingual Inclusive Education

- **Core Right:** Brazilian Sign Language (*Língua Brasileira de Sinais - Libras*) is recognized as an official linguistic and cultural system of expression. In bilingual schools and inclusive regular classrooms, Libras must be treated as the primary language (L1), with written Brazilian Portuguese taught and assessed as a secondary language (L2).
- **Mandatory Classroom Practice:**
  - Continuous presence of certified Libras translators and interpreters (*tradutores e intérpretes de Libras*) across all curricular and extracurricular activities.
  - Development of bilingual pedagogical materials designed from a visual-spatial cognitive perspective.
  - Evaluative criteria adapted for L2 Portuguese, recognizing that grammatical and syntactic structures in Libras differ fundamentally from Portuguese syntax.
- **Statutory Citations:**
  - **Lei Federal nº 10.436/2002:** Recognizes Libras as the legal means of communication and linguistic expression of deaf communities in Brazil.
  - **Lei Federal nº 9.394/1996 (LDB) — Capítulo V-A (*Da Educação Bilíngue de Surdos*), added by Lei Federal nº 14.191/2021:**
    - *Art. 60-A & Art. 60-B:* Institutionalizes Bilingual Education for the Deaf as an independent educational modality, defining Libras as L1 and written Portuguese as L2, starting from early childhood education through lifelong learning.
  - **Lei Federal nº 13.146/2015 (LBI):**
    - *Art. 28, Inciso IV & V:* Mandates bilingual education supply in Libras and written Portuguese, alongside certified interpreters and bilingual teachers.

---

### 6. National Book and Didactic Material Program (PNLD) Accessibility

- **Core Right:** The *Programa Nacional do Livro e do Material Didático (PNLD)* is mandated to ensure that all distributed didactic, pedagogical, and literary materials are available in accessible formats for students and teachers with disabilities.
- **Accessible Formats:** Accessible digital books (EPUB3 with accessible semantics), tactile Braille volumes, enlarged print editions with high chromatic contrast, and synchronized audio descriptions.
- **Statutory Citations:**
  - **Decreto Federal nº 9.099/2017:**
    - *Art. 25:* Establishes that the procurement and distribution of didactic materials under the PNLD must incorporate specific accessibility mechanisms, ensuring that students and educators with disabilities receive materials concurrently with other users in appropriate accessible formats.

## 🛡️ Implementation Invariants & Safety Standards

1. **Non-Diagnostic Constraint:** Every platform feature derived from these guidelines must assist teachers in making pedagogical and environmental accommodations. Never generate clinical screening classifications or diagnostic terminology.
2. **Language Segregation:** All statutory citations, legislative decrees, and legal naming conventions retain their authentic Brazilian Portuguese nomenclature (*LBI*, *LDB*, *AEE*, *Profissional de Apoio Escolar*, *Pranchas de Comunicação*), while internal technical architecture, types, code, and documentation remain in English. User-facing interfaces in `apps/web` must be strictly in Brazilian Portuguese (PT-BR).
3. **Low-Resource Resilience:** All tools must function in low-resource environments (e.g., support for printable black-and-white low-tech templates, offline-ready printable exports, and low-bandwidth web pages).
4. **Universal Design for Learning (UDL):** Features should avoid segregating students into isolated activities, prioritizing adaptations that benefit the entire classroom while securing required individual accommodations.
